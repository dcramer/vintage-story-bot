# Navigation

How a Seraph sees the ground and moves across it. The mod only senses and applies inputs; every route, memory and decision lives in Node. Mineflayer-pathfinder is the shape reference (goals, movement costs, partial paths, replan reasons), but it plans on a complete world the server hands the client. A Seraph plans on what its camera has seen, so it keeps memory at two resolutions and looks before it walks.

## Vision: what the eye reports, and why

A player's vision is one image per frame: every ray from the eye stops at the first thing it hits, and small things stop being recognisable with distance. Everything the mod reports about the world follows from that and nothing else, and it follows three rules.

- **No attention on the wire.** The mod reports what is in view; nothing has to be named before it can be seen. Node decides what matters (a search narrows memory by code or trait). There is no watch list and no on-demand scan: a filter by name would make a goal blind to what it did not ask for, and a query would let Node learn what the camera never showed.
- **Acuity instead of a name filter.** Beyond eight blocks a block is reported only if it is big enough to make out from where the bot stands: about 1.2° of angular size (`SceneGeometry.Resolves`), so a full block is resolved to about 48 blocks and a loose stone or a stick to about 16. That is a fact of the block's shape, it is what bounds the count, and it is more honest than seeing every named block at 64.
- **Terrain is columns, things are sightings.** The ground itself is bulk terrain, reported once per visible column (soil, rock, sand, gravel, snow, ice, water, lava, the leaves of a canopy, the grass on the ground: `Sight.Bulk`, a material-and-shape fact). Anything else standing on or within three cells of a visible surface is a sighting with its facts. Within eight blocks the surroundings pass sees every cell in every direction and reports its code, and non-bulk blocks there are sightings too.

What that costs and what it does not: the eye still fires about one ray per column (about 700 columns per sweep by day, 270 at night) plus one per candidate block, under the same two-millisecond tick budget; sightings reach Node as a delta since its last look (`seen` cursor), so an unfiltered view is a few hundred rows per sweep, not per sense. The known blind spot is vertical faces beyond eight blocks: the column sweep looks at each column's top surface, so a nugget on a cliff face at twenty blocks is not seen until the bot is near it. Hearing through walls within 16 blocks and full-circle awareness within 8 stay as cheap stand-ins for sound and peripheral vision.

## Perception layers (mod → Node)

| Layer | Wire | Range | Content | Refresh |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Surroundings | `sense` / `control_step` terrain deltas | 8-block disk, 3 down / 6 up | Exact collision boxes, trait words and block code per cell, camera-independent, line-of-sight-verified | ≤4 blocks every 0.5 s, rest every 1.5 s; changed cells invalidate at once |
| Far view | `sense` / `control_step` surface snapshot | Surroundings plus the client's real field of view, 64 by day down to 12 in the dark | One standing-surface sample per column: `y`, `ground|canopy|water|hazard`, block code; every column to 16, even to 32, multiples of four beyond | Sampled every tick while a controller listens, ≤2 ms/64 rays, 150 ms after the camera stops turning; snapshot = confirmed in the last 5 s inside the view; Node remembers a week |
| Entities and items | `sense` / `control_step` sightings delta | Seen inside the field of view to the light-limited radius; near within 8 all around; living entities heard within 16 all around | Key, code, point, how, time; item quantity | Every tick while a controller listens, ≤32 rays; in view while confirmed within 1 s; Node remembers entities 20 s, items 60 s |
| Blocks | same feed | Every non-bulk block within 8 all around; beyond that, on or within three cells of each visible surface column when big enough to make out | Key, code, point, facts (name, variant, growth), access | Found as the surroundings and the eye sweep, ≤512 per sweep; in view while confirmed within 8 s; Node remembers a week |

Every goal that looks for something composes one search (`support/search.ts`): the loop, its ordering of leads, the frontier and the food search are in [search](search.md).
| Memory reads | `sightings`, `block_at`, `terrain`, `route` | What the memories below hold | Controller-local; nothing is asked of the world | Any time; absent means unknown |

Rules that hold for every layer: perception is a snapshot of what the camera sees now, never a query, and memory is Node's alone; a sample exists only if a line of sight from the eye reached it; absent means unknown, never air; nothing below the visible surface, behind a ridge or in an unloaded chunk is reported; stale samples expire in the mod (surroundings 120 s, surface 5 s) while Node keeps what it saw. The mod never plans or chooses where to look; Node turns the head and remembers what came into view.

## Memory (Node, `src/runtime/navigation/`)

- `TerrainMemory` (`terrain.ts`): cells with absolute collision boxes from the near-field deltas, read as a block grid. A cell is a place to stand when it has a floor and a body's worth of known free cells above it; water, fire and unknown are walls. `moves(node)` lists the ways out: walk or step (up to 0.6) to any of eight neighbours, jump one block up or drop up to three along a cardinal, never a diagonal past something solid, never a drop beside water, extra cost beside water. `gapMoves` hops a one-cell hole as a last resort. `frontier(node)` is the unknown cells a move would need.
- `SurfaceMemory` (`surface.ts`): far-field columns from the vision feed keyed by world coordinates so views from different spots merge. Answers coarse neighbours, shoreline adjacency and nearest known column.
- `SightingsMemory` (`sightings.ts`): entities, items and blocks from the deltas, visible now (confirmed within the eye's window on its clock) or remembered as last seen (20 s entities, 60 s items, a week for blocks). `Fieldwork.scan` waits one sweep and reads it through `view`, narrowed by code; the `sightings` action reads the same view; threats read `nearbyEntities`, which the game client overlays from this memory on every state.
- All live on the game client and, for terrain, surface and blocks, on disk per world (`knowledge.ts`): loaded when the world's identifier is first observed, saved at most once a minute and on shutdown, kept for a week unless a block change is reported. The controller's eye loop refreshes them every 250 ms whether or not a goal is running. Entities and items are transient.

## Planning

Two planners, one contract: a route is a list of standing cells; every cell and every move between them came from observed blocks before the bot moves.

- **Rough route** (`planRoughRoute`): A* over far-view columns toward a goal. Edges: adjacent columns follow the fine limits (jump up one, drop three); coarser rings only bound average slope. Water and hazard are never nodes; canopy and shoreline cost extra; 16×16 areas that already failed on the ground are penalized by the caller. Status `success` (goal column reached), `partial` (best visible progress; walk it and look again), `noPath`. Output is simplified to bends and 12-block spans.
- **Fine route** (`findRoute`): A* over standing cells using `moves` and, only where no ordinary move leaves that way, `gapMoves`; hostile-avoidance radii; edges blocked after stalls; a 250 ms deadline and a 1024-node budget. A goal beyond what has been seen yields a partial route to the frontier cell nearest the goal, as mineflayer-pathfinder does, but never one reached down a drop the body could not climb back when it lies below both the start and the goal: a frontier that looks nearer from the bottom of a hole is a hole. The rough route keeps the same rule over columns. A pit is nothing special: cells the search cannot leave never reach the goal.

## Walking (`Fieldwork.walk` → `Navigation`)

1. Target within 12 blocks: one fine leg.
2. Farther: turn toward the target and wait until the vision feed stops adding columns; if no `success` rough route, also glance ±50°. Plan a rough route; hand the fine navigator the farthest checkpoint within 40 blocks as a leg (`horizontalOnly`, arrival radius from the sample spacing). Arrive, look again from the new viewpoint, repeat (≤8 legs) until the target is within the horizon, then walk the final fine leg.
3. Nothing visible leads there: `blocked/no_visible_route` beyond 48 blocks (the caller's stall recovery takes over: foliage clearance, exploration legs; no blind probes), otherwise one direct fine leg.

`Navigation.tick` follows a route like a player, one step at a time: it hands the mod the next checkpoint as a step (`control_step` with `toward`, `reach`, `reachY`, `hop`), and the mod walks it with its hand on the keys every game tick (`StepTracker`): turn to face the point, forward while roughly facing it (within 60° with more than a block to go, within 20° on the last block or for a hop) and supported, jump in water or, for a hop, once within 1.1 blocks and lined up, forward let go in the air on a drop so the body falls onto the cell below, and the step ends `arrived` (on the point, or past it, and standing), `blocked` (walking without getting closer for 0.7 s) or `expired`. Node reads the outcome, advances past the reached checkpoint, merges a run the body can walk without a turn into one point, gentle slopes included and up to seven blocks (undone if the body drifts off the line), hands the mod the cell after as well so a step down of a block is walked off in stride, sprints when fleeing or when satiety is over a third of the bar and the run ahead is worth it, never crouches, and re-plans at once on `blocked`, when the next cell stops being standable, or after 4 s without progress (the edge is then blocked for that goal). While walking a partial route it plans ahead: whenever the far view has filled in since the plan, it plans again from where the body is and takes the new route in stride if it gets farther, so the walk changes course without a stop at the old frontier. A cliff guard refuses to step toward any cell with nothing to stand on within a jump up or three blocks down unless it is the checkpoint itself. Nothing in the mod chooses the point or what comes next. `surveying` looks at the unknown cells a move would need for up to 2.5 s before planning. Terminal: `arrived`, `blocked` (`no_observed_route`, `exploration_exhausted`, `deadline`, `lost_support`, replan limit), `cancelled` (damage, death, session or control change, `control_lost`), `yielded` (caller policy).

## Policies

One-deep water over solid ground is waded (a `wet` node, costlier than dry ground; the follower holds jump while the feet are wet and never sneaks there); deeper water is swum (a `swim` node at the surface, costlier still, so dry ground wins whenever there is any; `map.swim` turns it off); fire never. No placing, doors, or falls over three blocks; beside water only a one-block step down. When the ground reachable from where the bot stands runs out within a few dozen cells, `travel` ends with reason `pit` and the `dig_out` goal cuts a staircase toward the destination (`digging.ts`). Being hurt is an event on the goal and the navigation view, not a stop; a life alert, death, lost controls or a changed session still are. Leaf clearing is a stuck recovery in `leaf-clearing.ts`, aimed at the real destination, never a routing primitive. Hostile avoidance (`threats.ts`) replaces the target with a flee point while a seen, heard or recently seen hostile is inside its radius and resumes afterwards; a hostile behind a ridge and out of earshot is unknown, as it is to a player.

## Observability

`goal_status` progress phases: `walking`, `rough_route` / `no_rough_route` (with rough route status and the chosen leg), `rerouting` (reason), `route_cleared`, `probing`, `recovering_route`, `evading`. The dashboard `navigation` topic carries the live navigator view: state, reason, next checkpoint, yaw error, last replan, diagnostics (missing cells, support, clearance), threats.

## Verification

Synthetic checks in a scratchpad cover the rough route planner (lake skirted, ramp taken, partial beyond what was seen), the fine planner (diagonals, wall corner, pillar) and the seeing walk loop. Live gameplay is the real test: run `travel` at terrain that used to get stuck, watch `navigation.diagnostics` and the rough route phases, and record the outcome in the handoff.
